What a valid invoice needs

The exact requirements depend on where you are and whether you are registered for VAT. Broadly, an invoice should let the client pay the right amount and let a tax authority check it later. That means:

  • A unique number that runs in sequence, so nobody can quietly reuse one.
  • Both parties clearly identified — legal name and address, not just a trading name.
  • What was supplied, with quantity and price per unit.
  • Dates — when it was issued and when it is due.
  • Tax — the rate, the amount and the total excluding tax.
  • How to pay — bank details with a reference that matches the invoice number.

UK VAT invoices

If you are VAT-registered, your VAT number must appear on the invoice, along with the VAT rate and the VAT amount in sterling. For a sale to another VAT-registered business in the EU, the reverse charge wording applies — put it in the tax note field. If you are not registered, you must not show VAT at all.

US sales tax

Sales tax is set at state and local level, so there is no single national rate. You collect it only where you have nexus — a physical presence, employees, or in many states enough sales volume. Services are taxable in some states and not others. Set your rate in the form and confirm your position with your state's revenue department.

Getting paid faster

  1. Send it the day you finish. Invoices age badly. A short email with the PDF attached and the due date in the subject line works better than a portal upload nobody checks.
  2. Use short terms. Fourteen days, not thirty, unless the client's system requires longer.
  3. Number everything. If a client asks for a credit note or a reissue, keep the sequence intact and reference the original.
  4. Chase on day one. A polite reminder on the due date is normal and effective. Statutory interest is the next step, not the first.
  5. Keep a copy. Download the PDF and store it with the contract. You will need it for your tax return.
Keep invoices for five years

UK businesses generally need to keep records for at least five years from the 31 January filing deadline. US records are typically kept for three to seven years depending on the document. The PDF you download here is the copy you keep.

Questions

What must a UK VAT invoice include?

A unique number, issue date, your business name, address and VAT number, the customer's name and address, a description of the supply, quantity, unit price, VAT rate, VAT amount in sterling, and the total excluding VAT. This generator adds the VAT fields when you enter a VAT number and pick a rate.

When should I not charge VAT?

If you are not VAT-registered, you must not charge it. Registration becomes compulsory once taxable turnover passes the current threshold in any rolling 12-month period; you can also register voluntarily. Some supplies are exempt or outside the scope of VAT.

What if the client is outside the UK?

Place of supply rules decide whether UK VAT applies. Services to business customers outside the UK are often outside the scope, with the reverse charge applying instead. Put the wording in the tax note and keep evidence of the client's location.

Can I add a late payment fee?

For UK business-to-business invoices, statutory interest and fixed compensation apply automatically under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998, provided your terms say payment is due within a set period. State the terms on the invoice.

Should the invoice be a PDF or a Word file?

A PDF is the safer choice for sending, because the layout cannot shift. Use the Word version when you need to edit wording or branding before sending.
