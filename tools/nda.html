When you actually need an NDA

Most freelancers overuse NDAs. If a client is paying you for work and the contract already has a confidentiality clause, a separate NDA adds nothing. The times it does matter:

  • Before a pitch, when you are sharing a concept, pricing model or proprietary method with someone who has not hired you yet.
  • Partnership or joint venture talks, where both sides reveal customer data, roadmaps or financials.
  • Due diligence, when a buyer or investor looks at your books.
  • Contractor onboarding, when someone gets access to client lists or source code.

In each case, the NDA protects specific information — not the general fact that you work in an industry. A clause that tries to stop someone competing with you is a non-compete, not an NDA, and it is often unenforceable.

What good confidentiality terms look like

This template balances four things. First, a clear definition of what counts as confidential, including the fact that the discussions themselves are confidential. Second, carve-outs for information that is already public, independently developed or lawfully received elsewhere — without these, the agreement is unreasonable and harder to enforce. Third, permitted disclosures to lawyers, accountants and regulators. Fourth, a finite term and a return-or-destroy obligation.

It also says that no intellectual property licence is granted. That matters: sharing a design concept under an NDA should not give the other side the right to use it.

Mutual or one-way?

This generator produces a mutual NDA, which is the right default for a conversation between two businesses. If only one side is disclosing — for example, you are receiving a client's confidential data — you want a one-way NDA instead. You can still use this document and delete the parts that apply to the other party, but a purpose-built one-way agreement is cleaner.

Keep a record of what you sent

An NDA only helps if you can show what information was covered and when. Keep the signed copy and the email or message that shared the material, with dates.

Questions

Mutual or one-way?

A mutual NDA binds both sides and suits partnership talks, joint pitches and early client conversations. A one-way NDA protects only the disclosing party, and is used when just one side shares information.

How long should it last?

Two to five years from the last disclosure is typical. Trade secrets may justify longer, but unusually long terms get negotiated and can look unreasonable. Three years is a sensible default.

Is it enforceable without a signature?

It can be, if you can show the other party accepted the terms — for example by continuing to receive confidential information after being told the terms apply. A signed copy is much easier to prove.

What should I leave out?

Do not restrict someone from working in their industry, hiring staff generally, or reporting a crime. Overbroad clauses are often unenforceable and undermine the rest of the agreement.

Does this replace a contract?

No. An NDA only covers confidentiality. You still need a services contract for scope, payment, IP ownership and termination.
