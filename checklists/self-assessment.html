The two dates that catch people out

Almost everyone knows the return is due on 31 January. What surprises people is the payment on account system. If your tax bill is over £1,000 and more than 20% of it is not collected at source, HMRC asks for half of next year's expected bill at the same time — also on 31 January — and the other half on 31 July.

That means a first-year freelancer can file a £3,000 bill and find £4,500 leaves their account, because the January payment includes the first instalment towards the following year. It is not a double charge; it is an advance. But it is the single most common cash-flow shock in self-employment.

If your income has dropped, you can ask HMRC to reduce your payments on account. Do that before the deadline, not after.

Expenses: what is allowed

The test is whether a cost is wholly and exclusively for the business. In practice that means ordinary running costs qualify — software, hosting, insurance, professional fees, travel between work sites, stock, subcontractors. Personal living costs do not, and neither does clothing that you could wear outside work or training that teaches a new trade.

Two rules of thumb. Keep every receipt, even for small amounts, because you cannot claim what you cannot show. And if a cost is mixed, claim the business proportion and be able to explain how you worked it out.

Records to keep, and for how long

Keep sales invoices, purchase receipts, bank statements and mileage logs for at least five years from the 31 January deadline. If you file late, or HMRC opens a check, the clock can run longer. Digital copies are fine as long as they are readable and complete.

Check the current rules

Thresholds, allowances and deadlines change. Confirm the figures on GOV.UK before you rely on them, especially the trading allowance, the VAT threshold and the payment on account rules.

Questions

What is the deadline?

Online return and any tax owed: 31 January after the tax year ends. Paper return: 31 October. Second payment on account: 31 July.

Do I need to register under £1,000?

Usually not — the trading allowance covers it. If you have other untaxed income, or want to protect your National Insurance record, the position can differ.

How long do I keep records?

At least five years from the 31 January filing deadline. HMRC can open a compliance check within that window.

What if I file late?

There is an automatic £100 penalty for missing the deadline, even if no tax is owed, with further penalties after three, six and twelve months. Paying late also accrues interest. Filing a rough return on time is usually cheaper than filing a perfect one late.
